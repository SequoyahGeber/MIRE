### D-215 · Chests are a five-rung rarity ladder, and the rung is legible before the UI is

**Context:** F-541. MIRE shipped seven loot tables but only two kinds of container ever reached a
map: free `Cache_` crates and the key-locked Gilded Chest. The three tables that were supposed to be
the coin economy — `small`, `bog`, `strongbox` — were three prices over three overlapping pools, and
`bog` and `strongbox` were placed by nothing at all. Coins had almost nothing to buy, and a player
looking at a container could not tell what it was worth.

**The decision:** one ladder, five rungs, named for what players already understand —
`basic`, `common`, `rare`, `epic`, `legendary`. Three properties are non-negotiable and each is
asserted by `tools/chest_placement_check.gd::_check_ladder()` against the real Hollowmere boot:

1. **Price strictly increases** — 10 / 30 / 75 / 150 / 300, roughly doubling. A rung that costs the
   same as its neighbour is not a rung.
2. **What the price buys is odds, not potency** (D-063). The POWERUP share of one weighted draw runs
   5% → 48% → 50% → 55% → 72%, and the *rarity* of the powerup lines climbs alongside it. The check
   computes the share from the authored weights, so re-tuning a `.tres` re-grades the ladder rather
   than silently drifting from this entry.
3. **Every rung owns its own silhouette and its own locator tint.** Width climbs monotonically
   (0.62 → 1.12 m) and no two palettes share anything. The price a player pays has to be legible at
   the moment of SIGHTING, because that is when the decision to walk over is made; a ladder you have
   to open a UI to read is a table, not a ladder.

**Scarcity carries price too.** Per island: 5 basic, 4 common, 3 rare, 2 epic, 1 legendary, and on
the procedural map the same shape as climbing PoiDef `min_spacing_m`. `validate()` fails the build
if any rung comes up short — a missing rung is a hole in the ladder, not a quieter map.

**What stayed off the ladder:** `gilded` (Gilded Key), `wellspring` (granted on a cap), `sunken`
(risk-priced), `boss` (kill reward). None of them are bought, so none of them is a price. `gilded`
wears the `legendary` mesh deliberately — both are "the chest you saved up for", and two different
expensive silhouettes would teach a distinction the loot behind them does not make.

**Free caches are not a sixth tier.** `Cache_<n>` markers build the `basic` TABLE at cost 0; the
price comes from the marker prefix, not the tier. Muck's proven loop is the reason: free caches seed
the coins that priced chests spend, and a run whose first container wants 10 coins the player cannot
have is a run that opens on a locked door.

**Rejected:** keeping `small`/`bog`/`strongbox` alongside the new tables. Two parallel ladders
sharing one Registry namespace is how the first one rotted — a table nothing places looks authored
right up until someone counts.
